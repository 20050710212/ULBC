## 1    Introduction

<a id="P008"></a>

This contribution addresses objective performance requirements and design constraints for ULBC VAD/DTX/CNG operation. The proposals made here rest on the assumption that DTX will be the default mode of ULBC operation in both link directions, i.e. in uplink and in downlink.

<a id="P009"></a>

Clause 2 substantiates this assumption, discusses what follows from it for the qualification and selection testing, and addresses the two parameters that govern the efficiency of DTX operation: the Active Frame Ratio (AFR) and the SID update interval. For the AFR it is proposed to take AMR-WB at 12.65 kbit/s on clean speech as the reference rather than the AFR of a legacy codec measured in background noise, and for the SID update interval concrete values are proposed. This results in Proposals 1 to 4.

<a id="P010"></a>

Clause 3 turns these into concrete text updates, for clause 5.1 of the ULBC Performance requirements (ULBC-3) [1] and for the DTX/CNG design constraint of the ULBC Design constraints (ULBC-4) [3], which are put forward as Proposal 5.

<a id="P011"></a>

## 2    Discussion

<a id="P012"></a>

## 2.1    DTX as the default mode of operation

<a id="P013"></a>

## 2.1.1    Uplink

<a id="P014"></a>

Several independent considerations suggest that ULBC will be operated with VAD/DTX/CNG enabled by default in the uplink:

<a id="P015"></a>

Power consumption: for the battery-constrained devices targeted by ULBC, the radio transmit chain is a dominant contributor to the power budget during a call. Suppressing transmission during inactive speech segments reduces the transmit duty cycle roughly in proportion to the speech inactivity factor, which in conversational speech is typically in the order of 40%.

<a id="P016"></a>

Thermal behaviour: reducing the transmit duty cycle also lowers sustained dissipation and hence the surface temperature of small form-factor devices, where the thermal budget rather than the battery may be the binding constraint; see also the analysis in [1].

<a id="P017"></a>

Precedent and quality neutrality: VAD/DTX/CNG is specified and deployed as the normal mode of operation for all 3GPP speech codecs (AMR, AMR-WB, EVS). Long-standing experience with these codecs shows that, when properly designed, DTX operation is possible virtually without affecting the resulting voice service quality.

<a id="P018"></a>

## 2.1.2    Downlink

<a id="P019"></a>

The same conclusion holds for the downlink, for partly different reasons:

<a id="P020"></a>

End-to-end consequence of uplink DTX: where ULBC is used at both ends, uplink DTX at the sending UE already results in a discontinuous packet stream towards the receiving UE. Restoring a continuous stream would require a network entity to regenerate or transcode the inactive segments, adding complexity and delay without benefit. In calls towards a fixed endpoint, such as an emergency call to a call centre, the downlink ULBC stream is instead generated by a network-side encoder. Also in that case there is no reason to encode and transmit inactive segments continuously. Downlink DTX is therefore the expected mode of operation in either configuration.

<a id="P021"></a>

Power saving at the receiving UE: fewer received packets reduce receiver and decoder activity and increase the sleep opportunities that can be exploited by connected-mode DRX.

<a id="P023"></a>

Observation: ULBC will typically run with DTX enabled while continuous transmission is expected to be the exception.

<a id="P024"></a>

## 2.2    Consequences for the performance requirements and the selection testing

<a id="P025"></a>

If DTX is the default, or at least the main, mode of operation, then the ULBC candidates have to be assessed predominantly in that mode. The following consequences for the ULBC performance requirements and for the qualification and selection testing are proposed:

<a id="P026"></a>

Proposal 1: Qualification and selection testing should focus on VAD/DTX/CNG active, so that the candidates are compared in the mode in which ULBC will actually be operated. Testing with VAD/DTX/CNG inactive should have lower priority, especially if qualification and selection testing bandwidth is an issue. It is proposed to reflect this proposal by adding a corresponding note beneath the performance requirement tables in section 4 of the ULBC Performance requirements (ULBC-3) [1].

<a id="P028"></a>

Proposal 2: The Active Frame Ratio (AFR) in DTX operation and the SID/CNG update rate are subject to objective performance requirements and design constraints, respectively.

<a id="P029"></a>

## 2.3    Reference for the ULBC AFR requirement in DTX operation

<a id="P030"></a>

The Active Frame Ratio (AFR), i.e. the percentage of frames that are neither SID nor NO DATA as defined in the present version of ULBC Performance requirements (ULBC-3) [1], is the natural objective measure of the effectiveness of DTX operation. The question is which reference the ULBC AFR requirement should be derived from.

<a id="P031"></a>

Contrary to the present draft text in Pdoc ULBC3, it is proposed not to derive the requirement from the AFR of AMR-WB or EVS measured on noisy speech. The VADs of these codecs are deliberately conservative: in the presence of certain background noise types they classify a large fraction of the inactive frames as active, which may push the AFR in such cases to very high figures. This behaviour forfeits much of the bit rate saving that DTX is meant to deliver. For the background noise categories (car, office and street) for which requirements for noisy speech performance are expected to be defined, such conservative VAD behaviour is undesirable for ULBC, as it operates under considerably tighter power management constraints than the legacy codecs from which this behaviour is inherited.

<a id="P032"></a>

Instead, the AFR of AMR-WB at 12.65 kbit/s measured on clean speech, i.e. on the same speech items before the background noise is added, is proposed as the reference. This reference reflects the speech and pause structure of the test material rather than the noise robustness of a legacy VAD, and it is well defined and reproducible. It assumes that the background noise is added to the items only after the reference AFR has been determined.

<a id="P033"></a>

In order to keep the requirement reasonable and give ULBC proponents sufficient design freedom to optimize speech in background noise quality despite such an ‘ideal’ reference, a tolerance on top of this reference is proposed.

<a id="P034"></a>

Proposal 3: The ULBC AFR requirement for DTX operation, for clean and noisy speech shall be the AFR of AMR-WB at 12.65 kbit/s measured on the corresponding clean speech items, plus a tolerance of [x] percentage points.

<a id="P035"></a>

## 2.4    SID update rate in DTX operation

<a id="P036"></a>

While the AFR characterises how often active frames are transmitted, the second parameter that determines the efficiency of DTX operation is the rate at which SID/CNG updates are sent during inactive segments. Each SID update re-activates the transmit chain, so an unnecessarily high update rate erodes the power and traffic savings that DTX is intended to deliver, whereas an excessively low rate limits the ability of the CNG to track changes of the background noise.

<a id="P037"></a>

The traffic analysis in [2] investigates SID/DTX traffic patterns for ULBC and provides a basis for selecting suitable SID update intervals for the two ULBC operating points. Taking that analysis as the reference, the following SID update intervals are proposed as design constraints:

<a id="P038"></a>

for the 1.0 kbit/s operating point, a SID update interval of [640] ms, i.e. one SID update every four packets/bundles;

<a id="P039"></a>

for the 1.4 kbit/s operating point, a SID update interval of [480] ms.

<a id="P040"></a>

The figures are put in brackets to indicate that some flexibility remains and that the final values may be refined in the light of further analysis and discussion.

<a id="P041"></a>

Proposal 4: The ULBC SID update interval in DTX operation shall be [640] ms at 1.0 kbit/s and [480] ms at 1.4 kbit/s, in line with the traffic analysis in [2].

Applying Proposal 4, it is proposed to specify the SID update interval in that design constraint. The proposed update of the corresponding row is shown below. Given the discussion and the proposals of the present contribution, the editor's note attached to that row is considered obsolete and is proposed to be deleted in its entirety.
