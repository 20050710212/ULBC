Title:    Considerations on the Use of Non-Stationary Noise in ULBC Testing

<a id="P001"></a>

Source:    Panasonic Holdings Corporation

<a id="P002"></a>

Document for:    Discussion & Agreement

<a id="P003"></a>

Agenda item:    4.8

<a id="P005"></a>

Introduction

<a id="P006"></a>

TR 26.940 notes that background audio may contain relevant information, particularly in emergency calls, and that removing all background audio may not always be desirable [1].

<a id="P007"></a>

ULBC-3 identifies Car, Office, and Street as noisy-speech conditions. Among these conditions, Street and Office noise are categorized as non-stationary noise [2].

<a id="P008"></a>

This contribution proposes to clarify the use of non-stationary noise in ULBC testing.

<a id="P009"></a>

Discussion

<a id="P012"></a>

Non-stationary noise varies in level or acoustic characteristics over time and may contain distinct acoustic events, such as vehicle pass-by sounds in Street noise and telephone ringing in Office noise. Street noise may be used to represent an outdoor street environment. Where road traffic is part of the intended scene, traffic events such as vehicle pass-by sounds should be present not only in the source recording but also in the segments actually used for evaluation. An arbitrarily selected segment may not contain the events that characterize the intended non-stationary noise condition. It is therefore important to confirm that the selected evaluation segments contain the intended events and that the same segments are used consistently for candidate and reference processing.

<a id="P013"></a>

Proposal

<a id="P014"></a>

It is proposed that the applicable ULBC selection and characterization test and processing plans clarify the use of non-stationary noise as follows:

<a id="P015"></a>

When a non-stationary noise condition is tested, the evaluation segments shall contain the noise events that characterize the intended acoustic scene, such as vehicle pass-by sounds for Street noise. The same event-bearing segments shall be used consistently for candidate and reference processing.

<a id="P016"></a>

Conclusion

<a id="P017"></a>

The proposed clarification is intended to improve the reproducibility of the evaluation conditions and to ensure that relevant non-stationary noise events are represented in the evaluation segments. The Audio Ad Hoc meeting is invited to agree that this clarification be reflected in the applicable ULBC test and processing plans.
