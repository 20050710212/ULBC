Introduction
At the TSG SA#112 plenary meeting, the ULBC-MED WID in SP-260696 was approved [1], which includes the following objective related to the RTP header:
Specification of an RTP payload format for the ULBC codec and determination of essential fields for RTP header reduction.
The present document is the Permanent Document ULBC-9 of the ULBC-MED work item. It captures the working assumptions and solutions and consolidations on RTP header reduction and the related system integration aspects for IMS voice over NB-IoT(GEO). The consolidations captured in the present document will be reflected in the ULBC RTP payload format specification.
Transport of GEO voice and applicability of RTP header reduction
This clause captures the transport options agreed by SA2 for GEO voice and identifies in which case the RTP header needs to be reduced.
Scope
The present document studies support of the following packet types for IMS voice over NB-IoT(GEO):
ULBC voice (active speech) packets at all operating points defined by the ULBC_Codec work item;
Silence/DTX/CNG (SID) packets generated by the ULBC codec;
Support of Dual-Tone Multi-Frequency (DTMF);
and if identified, studies SDP offer/answer signalling for the codec(s) for IMS voice over NB-IoT NTN, in alignment with TS 26.114 [5] and in coordination with C-T1 (TS 24.229).
Transport options and protocol overhead
Two transport configurations are relevant to the ULBC media stream, in line with the stage-2 architecture agreed by SA2 :
IP-based transport.
UHRR-enabled transport.
Table 1 compares the protocol header overhead of the transport configurations for the ULBC codec operating at 1.0 kbit/s with 80 ms and 160 ms packetization. Percentages are relative to the 1.0 kbit/s gross codec bitrate.
Table 1: Protocol header overhead per transport configuration (ULBC at 1.0 kbit/s)
Protocol configuration | Header size per packet | Header overhead bitrate, 80 ms packetization | Header overhead bitrate, 160 ms packetization
IPv6/UDP/RTP, uncompressed | 60 octets | 6.0 kbit/s (600%) | 3.0 kbit/s (300%)
IPv4/UDP/RTP, uncompressed | 40 octets | 4.0 kbit/s (400%) | 2.0 kbit/s (200%)
IP-based transport with ROHC (steady state, NOTE 1) | ~3–4 octets | ~0.3–0.4 kbit/s (30–40%) | ~0.15–0.2 kbit/s (15–20%)
UHRR-enabled transport, uncompressed RTP header | 12 octets | 1.2 kbit/s (120%) | 0.6 kbit/s (60%)
UHRR-enabled transport, reduced RTP header | FFS | FFS | FFS
NOTE 1:    ROHC steady-state second-order state; context initialization and repair use larger context-establishing packets. ROHC operates per link and is applicable to the IP-based transport only.
For the IP-based transport, ROHC reduces the header overhead in steady state, but context establishment and repair between the link-local compressor and decompressor are penalized by the GEO one-way delay in the order of 250–300 ms: context damage leads to extended packet discard or context refresh with full headers.
For the UHRR-enabled transport, no IP or UDP header remains on the radio link; however, the uncompressed 12-octet RTP header alone corresponds to 60% of the codec rate at 160 ms packetization and 120% at 80 ms packetization. RTP header reduction is therefore necessary for the UHRR-enabled transport to preserve the payload budget available to the codec.
RTP header field analysis
RTP is defined in IETF RFC 3550 [4]. In a typical two-way voice call, the first 12 bytes of the RTP header are mandatory; the CSRC list and header extension are optional and not used in a point-to-point voice session. Each field serves a different function for audio transport, and different features rely on different fields, as summarised in Table 1 based on TS 26.114 [5] and related RTP payload specifications.
Table 2: RTP header field analysis
RTP header field | Size (bits) | Function (RFC 3550) | Analysis under the agreed service scope | Classification
version (V) | 2 | RTP version; always 2 for RFC 3550. | Constant for all packets of the session. The value is known a priori by both endpoints. | Static, can be removed
padding (P) | 1 | Indicates presence of padding octets at the end of the payload. | Padding is not required for speech payloads in the agreed service scope. If the absence of padding is established for the session, the field is invariant. | Static, can be removed
extension (X) | 1 | Indicates presence of an RTP header extension. | No RTP header extension is identified as necessary within the service scope. If the absence of header extensions is established for the session, the field is invariant. | Static, can be removed
CSRC count (CC) | 4 | Number of CSRC identifiers following the fixed header. | The media path is point-to-point between the UE and the network media endpoint; no RTP mixers contribute to the stream on this leg. CC is always 0 and no CSRC list is present. | Static, can be removed
marker (M) | 1 | Profile-defined; for audio, typically set on the first packet after a silence interval (talk-spurt onset). | Depends on deployment scenario and implementation aspects. | FFS
payload type (PT) | 7 | Identifies the RTP payload format. | A single ULBC payload type per media transport session is established in SDP; the value is invariant. NOTE: additional payload types (e.g. DTMF events per RFC 4733, FEC per RFC 8627) would require the PT information to be retained in some form; their applicability within the reduced-header service scope is FFS. | FFS
sequence number (SN) | 16 | Increments by one per packet; used for loss detection and sequence restoration. | Packet loss detection remains necessary over the GEO radio link. However, the new information per packet is limited to a small increment; long-term ambiguity can be resolved from session state. The full 16-bit field need not be carried in every packet. | FFS
timestamp (TS) | 32 | Sampling instant of the first octet of the media data. | With a fixed codec frame length and a fixed packetization/bundling time, the TS advances by a constant nominal increment per packet and is largely derivable from the SN during continuous transmission. Discontinuities (e.g. across DTX gaps, if applicable) require explicit signalling of the increment. | FFS
SSRC | 32 | Identifies the synchronization source; enables demultiplexing of multiple RTP streams and collision detection. | A single RTP stream is carried per media transport session, and the stream identity is unambiguously provided by the transport session / bearer context. SSRC-based demultiplexing and collision detection are not required on the point-to-point leg. | FFS
CSRC list | 32 per entry | Identifies contributing sources inserted by mixers. | Not present, since CC = 0 (see above). | Removed
Design guidelines and solutions
Design guidelines
For the reduced RTP header, the following design guidelines and principles are applied to this study for IMS voice over NB-IoT(GEO):
- A single mono speech stream (one audio media description) per media transport session;
- A point-to-point media path between the UE and the network media endpoint on the leg where the reduced header applies; - No RTP mixers or translators contribute to the stream on this leg;
- A single ULBC payload format per session, with payload format parameters established in SDP at session setup;
- A fixed codec frame length with fixed packetization/bundling times (80 ms or 160 ms).
- SRTP integrity protection is not supported.
- RTCP is FFS.
- FEC is FFS.

Annex-A: Background of RTP header reduction
As specified in Clause 4.13 of TS 23.401 [2], IMS voice over NB-IoT (GEO) supports two mechanisms:
1. IP-based (mandatory):
IMS signalling and voice are transported as IP packets, with RoHC recommended for header compression. The protocol stack over the radio is ROHC (RTP/UDP/IP) / PDCP / RLC / MAC. In the RoHC steady state (Profile 1, RTP/UDP/IP), the 40-byte RTP/UDP/IP headers can be compressed to 1–3 bytes [8], resulting in an overall protocol overhead of about 8 bytes (3 + 1 + 1 + 3).
Figure A.1: Protocol stack from UE to IMS-AGW: IP-based transport with ROHC (no UHRR)
2. UHRR-enabled (optional):
UHRR optionally removes and restores the UDP/IP headers at the UE/PDN GW. The RTP header can also be reduced and restored at the UE/AGW. In this case, the protocol stack is RTP / PDCP / RLC / MAC. The 12-byte RTP header is transmitted with further reduction or compression.
